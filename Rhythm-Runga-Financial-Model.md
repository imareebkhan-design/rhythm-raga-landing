## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Rhythm Runga is a **structurally viable but currently sub-scale** business. The unit economics are positive — every student contributes ₹2,000/month toward fixed costs — but the student base is roughly **one-third of the size required to break even**.

The three findings that matter:

**Finding 1 — This is a volume problem, not a pricing problem or a cost problem.**
At 14 students, even raising fees to ₹5,000 and cutting instructor payout to 40% would still leave you losing ₹33,000/month. No amount of margin engineering fixes a 14-student academy. You need students. Everything else is second-order until you are past ~35 students.

**Finding 2 — The 50% per-student instructor payout is the biggest structural constraint in the business.**
It caps your gross margin at exactly 50% forever, and it means group classes give you *no* economics advantage. Six students in one room paying ₹4,000 each generates the same ₹2,000/student as six students taught individually — but costs the instructor one hour instead of six. You are paying for scale you don't receive. Switching group classes to a **per-hour instructor rate** lifts contribution per student from ₹2,000 to ~₹3,467 and drops break-even from **38 students to 22**. This is the highest-leverage change available to you and it requires zero new students. See Section 11.

**Finding 3 — Churn will decide whether you ever get there.**
The Conservative growth scenario (3 new students/month, 8% monthly churn) **never reaches break-even**. It asymptotes at 32–37 students and stalls there permanently. That is not a slow path to profit — it is a treadmill. The mathematical ceiling of any acquisition rate is `new students ÷ churn rate`. At 3 new and 8% churn, that ceiling is 37.5 — one student below break-even. You would work for eighteen months and end up losing ₹11,000/month.

**The path:** Base case (6 new students/month, 6% churn) reaches break-even in **month 6**, ₹25,000/month profit in **month 9**, ₹50,000/month in **month 14**. That is the realistic plan. It requires roughly **50 qualified leads per month** at base-case conversion rates.

**Do not start Google Ads yet.** Your maximum defensible CAC is currently unknowable because you have no retention data. Provisionally cap it at **₹4,000** (a 6-month assumed student lifetime at 2:1 LTV:CAC). At an assumed cost-per-lead of ₹312 and 12% lead-to-paid conversion, ads look workable — but that 12% is a guess, and marketing spend is a *fixed* cost that raises your break-even. ₹20,000/month of ads moves break-even from 38 students to 48. Prove the funnel converts with free and cheap channels for 30 days first.

## 3. UNIT ECONOMICS — THE ECONOMICS OF ONE STUDENT

| Metric | Monthly | Annual (12 mo) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Student fee (revenue) | ₹4,000 | ₹48,000 |

| **Contribution** | **₹2,000** | **₹24,000** |
| Contribution margin | **50.0%** | 50.0% |

### Formula

```
Contribution per student = Fee × (1 − Instructor payout %)
                         = ₹4,000 × (1 − 0.50)
                         = ₹2,000
```

### What one additional student does

Every student you add puts **exactly ₹2,000/month** against your ₹75,000 fixed cost wall. Nothing else changes — rent doesn't move, electricity barely moves.

- 1 extra student = ₹2,000/month = ₹24,000/year
- Closing the 24-student gap to break-even = **₹48,000/month = ₹5,76,000/year swing**
- Below 38 students, each new student reduces your loss by ₹2,000
- Above 38 students, each new student is ₹2,000 of **pure profit**

This is the crossover that makes the business work: past break-even, contribution margin (50%) and *incremental* net margin (100% of contribution) diverge violently. At 100 students your net margin is 31%. At 14 it is −84%. Same business, same prices — only volume changed.

**⚠️ Caveat that materially matters:** this assumes fixed costs stay at ₹75,000 as you scale. **They will not.** At 60–100 students you will need more rooms, more hours, likely an admin person, and more power. See Section 10 and Section 14.

### How many students can you afford to lose?

At any student count `S` with churn rate `c`, you must acquire **more than `S × c`** just to stand still.

| Current students | Monthly churn 6% | Students lost/month | Minimum new students to not shrink |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 14 | 6% | 0.8 | 1 |
| 25 | 6% | 1.5 | 2 |
| 38 | 6% | 2.3 | 3 |
| 50 | 6% | 3.0 | 3 |
| 60 | 6% | 3.6 | 4 |
| 88 | 6% | 5.3 | 6 |

**The trap:** at 88 students with 6% churn you must acquire 6 students/month *forever* just to stay at 88. Acquisition never gets easier — it gets harder in absolute terms as you grow. This is why retention outranks acquisition once you're past break-even.

## 11. THE BIGGEST FINANCIAL LEVERS — RANKED

### 🥇 #1 — Class format & instructor payment structure *(the lever you probably haven't considered)*

**This is the highest-leverage change in the entire business and it needs zero new students.**

Under the current 50%-per-student model, group classes deliver **no economic benefit**. Six students in one room paying ₹4,000 each = ₹12,000 to the instructor for one hour of work, and ₹2,000/student to you — identical to six individual lessons.

**Switch group classes to a per-hour instructor rate.** Assumed rate: ₹800/session, 4 sessions/month = ₹3,200/month per class slot.

| Group size | Monthly revenue | Instructor cost | Contribution | **Contribution/student** | vs. ₹2,000 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 (current) | ₹4,000 | ₹2,000 | ₹2,000 | ₹2,000 | — |
| 4 | ₹16,000 | ₹3,200 | ₹12,800 | **₹3,200** | **+60%** |

| 8 | ₹32,000 | ₹3,200 | ₹28,800 | **₹3,600** | **+80%** |

**Break-even at 6-student groups: ₹75,000 ÷ ₹3,467 = 22 students.**

**You are 8 students away from break-even, not 24.**

⚠️ The ₹800/session rate is **my assumption** — I don't know your instructors' contracts or what they'd accept. But even at ₹1,500/session the maths works overwhelmingly. **This is the first question you should answer this week.**
